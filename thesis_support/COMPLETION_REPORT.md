## 2. Title, questions and objectives

**Title.** Simulation-Based Decision Support for Warehouse Workforce Planning and Order
Sequencing under Differentiated Service-Level Constraints.

**Research design.** Applied simulation-based experimental research. Design Science Research was
considered and rejected: there were no formal design cycles or stakeholder evaluation rounds to
justify it.

**Research questions.** RQ1 what discrete-event simulation adds to monthly capacity planning
over an analytical estimate; RQ2 how sequencing policies affect differentiated service and cost;
RQ3 how bottleneck diagnostics and adaptive capacity improve explainability; RQ4 how one
framework serves retrospective and forecast planning. All four are answered explicitly in
Chapter 7.

**General objective.** To design, implement and evaluate a simulation-based decision-support
framework recommending monthly workforce capacity and a sequencing policy under differentiated
service-level and economic constraints. Five specific objectives are stated in §1.4 and their
attainment is addressed in §7.1.

## 5. Strongest findings

1. **The analytical capacity estimate over-sized the workforce in all twelve months**, by up to
   nine FTE in December (~19% of the recommended workforce). Two mechanisms: independent
   per-stage rounding, and a substitution between capacity and operating discipline that no
   aggregate formula can represent.
2. **FIFO satisfied both service floors at none of the sixteen candidate workforces in the four
   campaign months.** Its failure is structural, not a resourcing problem.
3. **At December's recommended configuration, RL-3 was the only feasible policy**, holding the
   normal class at 85.4% where strict priority dropped it to 73.8%, at 13.2% lower cost.
   Expressed in capacity terms, strict priority needed **seven more FTE** to reach feasibility.
4. **Policy value is capacity-regime dependent**: at October `s11_7_5` the three policies differ
   in total cost by EUR 30.
5. **Picking was the primary pressure location in all twelve months**, accounting for 83.7–100%
   of late orders' accumulated waiting — though in three months it was *not* the most-utilised
   stage, which is reported and explained.
6. **The adaptive search rejected the additional FTE in all nine months it ran.** An FTE costs
   EUR 2,400/month; in six months it avoided under EUR 130 of penalties.
7. **Backlog and utilisation are nearly invariant to policy**, confirming that sequencing
   reallocates lateness rather than creating capacity.

## 6. Two assumptions that were corrected by verification

Both were caught before they reached the text, and both are documented in the thesis:

1. **"Urgent-First" is not urgent-first-then-FIFO.** SimPy's priority item compares only the
   priority field, so equal-priority orders leave the queue in binary-heap order. Demonstrated
   empirically (`verify_priority_store.py`): inserting 1…10 dequeues as 1, 3, 7, 10, 9, 6, 8, 5,
   2, 4. The class-level priority *is* strictly respected. This is documented as implemented, not
   corrected, and its consequences are analysed in §6.2 and Appendix F.
2. **The adaptive-search acceptance rule is not pure cost minimisation.** It has three branches,
   with feasibility taking precedence over cost. An earlier draft of Chapter 4 described only
   the cost branch; the description was corrected after reading `capacity_search.py`.

## 7. Limitations stated in the thesis

Synthetic data provenance · no external calibration or validation against a real warehouse · no
warehouse physics (travel, layout, congestion, batching, blocking) · operating-time abstraction
is not wall-clock · monthly FTE rather than rosters · service thresholds on the operating clock ·
single demand realisation per month retrospectively · forecast mode covers one month · the two
runs use different economic parameters and are never compared in absolute money · heuristic and
uncalibrated Stage Pressure weights · bounded candidate search, not optimisation · the two
compared engines differ in within-class queue mechanics · **the RL checkpoint is absent, so RL-3
results could not be regenerated**.
